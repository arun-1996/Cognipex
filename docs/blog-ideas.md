# Blog Ideas

## Purpose and status

This document is a future editorial backlog for Cogpace. It records possible
article directions; it does not approve research, drafting, publication, new
categories, or new Cognitive Domain terms. Every selected idea must follow the
scope, evidence, site-owner review, accessibility, maintenance, and medical-claim
requirements in `content-standards.md`.

Expansion beyond the first article remains subject to the validation and scaling
gate in the project roadmap.

## Editorial principles

- Help a general reader understand cognition in plain, inclusive language.
- Prefer useful explanations and evidence-aware guidance over performance
  promises, trends, or fear-based framing.
- Clearly distinguish established findings, mixed evidence, and practical
  inference.
- Treat Cogpace games as optional examples of task practice, never as clinical
  assessments or guaranteed ways to improve intelligence or daily functioning.
- Use the shared Cognitive Domain taxonomy only when the relationship is
  meaningful and the term has been editorially approved.

## Possible article series

### “Know the task”

Short explainers paired with an existing Cogpace game. Each article should
describe the task, common sources of variation, appropriate interpretation, and
what the score cannot establish.

### “Cognition in context”

Evidence-aware articles about factors that may influence task performance, such
as sleep, interruptions, stress, practice, environment, and device conditions.
These require especially careful causal and medical wording.

### “Read the evidence”

Plain-language research-literacy articles that teach readers how to assess
cognitive-performance and brain-training claims without endorsing a particular
commercial claim.

## Selection criteria

Prioritize an idea when it answers a clear reader question, can be supported by
authoritative and reviewable evidence, fits the current content model, adds value
beyond existing articles, and can be maintained. Defer ideas that depend on
unapproved product features, create avoidable medical risk, or imply benefits
beyond the available evidence.
