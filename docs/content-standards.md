# Content Standards

## Status and responsibility

These standards govern educational articles published by Cogpace. Cogpace is
independently created and maintained, and the site owner is responsible for the
accuracy, clarity, accessibility, and final publication of every article.
Automated tools may assist with research or drafting, but responsibility for the
published content remains with the site owner.

Health-related content remains general education. Topics that would require
individual diagnosis, treatment guidance, or qualified clinical or legal review
that is not available must remain out of scope.

## Evidence and citation standard

Each article must:

- cite at least two authoritative sources, including a peer-reviewed review,
  systematic review, or meta-analysis when one is available;
- prefer primary research, scholarly reviews, recognized professional guidance,
  and official public-health sources over commercial summaries;
- link each material scientific claim to its supporting source and record the
  author, title, publication, year, and DOI or stable URL;
- label associations as associations and avoid implying causation;
- distinguish established evidence, disputed or mixed evidence, and practical
  inference in plain language;
- represent meaningful limitations, uncertainty, and conflicting findings; and
- avoid citation laundering, unsupported statistics, and sources that cannot be
  independently inspected.

## Site-owner review workflow

1. **Scope:** define the reader, learning goal, related published content, and
   claims that are out of scope.
2. **Draft:** write in semantic WordPress blocks with citations placed near the
   claims they support.
3. **Evidence check:** verify every material claim against its cited source and
   represent uncertainty and study limitations accurately.
4. **Content check:** review structure, plain language, inclusive terminology,
   internal links, and the medical-claim boundary.
5. **Accessibility check:** review heading order, descriptive link text, image
   alternatives, keyboard access, reflow, contrast, and readability.
6. **Publish:** record the source-check and final-review date before publishing.
7. **Maintain:** set a next review date and revise, retain with an update note, or
   withdraw content when the evidence or product changes.

## Update cadence

Check educational articles at least every 12 months and sooner when a cited
source is corrected or retracted, an important new review changes the evidence,
a linked Cogpace experience changes, or a reader reports a substantive issue.
Correct material errors promptly and revise or withdraw content that is no longer
reliable.

## Accessibility requirements

Articles use one descriptive page title followed by correctly nested headings,
short paragraphs, lists where they improve comprehension, and descriptive link
text. Meaning must not depend on color, position, animation, or imagery alone.
Images require useful alternative text unless they are decorative. Tables need
headers and should be avoided when a list is clearer. Reading and interaction
must remain usable at 200% zoom and with keyboard-only navigation.

## Medical-claim boundary

Cogpace articles are general education, not medical advice or cognitive
assessment. They must not diagnose a reader, recommend treatment, promise to
prevent or cure a condition, or claim that a game will improve intelligence,
health, school performance, work performance, or everyday functioning without an
appropriate evidence, clinical, and legal basis.

When training evidence is limited, mixed, task-specific, or short-term, say so.
A score from a Cogpace activity must never be presented as a clinical result or
as evidence about an individual's cognitive health.
